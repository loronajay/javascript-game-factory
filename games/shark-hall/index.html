House rules.

Standard 8-ball as this table plays it. The last section is the one worth reading — it is where the house differs from the hall down the road.

The object

Fifteen balls: solids 1–7, stripes 9–15, and the 8, which belongs to nobody. Claim a group, pot all seven of it, then pot the 8. First to do that wins the rack.

The break

  • The breaker keeps shooting if a ball drops, but the break never assigns groups — the table stays open no matter what falls.
  • Pot the 8 on the break and it is a rerack: not a win, not a loss. Same breaker, fresh triangle.
  • Scratch on the break and it is an ordinary scratch — the incoming player takes ball in hand behind the head string.

The open table

  • Until someone claims a group the table is open, and you may strike any ball first except the 8.
  • Groups are settled by the first legal pot after the break. Not by the break, and never off a foul.
  • Drop one of each on that shot and the group is taken from whichever fell first, not from the majority.

A legal shot

Three things have to be true:

  • Hit your own first. Once you have a group, the first ball the cue ball touches must be from it. On an open table, anything but the 8. Once your group is gone, the 8 and nothing else.
  • Then a pot or a rail. After that contact, either a ball goes down or some ball — any ball, the cue ball included — must reach a cushion.
  • Don't scratch. The cue ball stays on the table.

Meet all three and pot one of yours, and you shoot again. Meet all three and pot nothing — or pot only the opponent's — and the turn simply passes, with no penalty. That one is a safety, and it is a real shot rather than a mistake.

Fouls, and what they cost

Every foul hands the incoming player ball in hand anywhere on the table — except a scratch. These are the five, named exactly as the turn card names them:

  • Scratch — the cue ball went down. The one exception to the penalty: ball in hand behind the head string only. The line itself is legal; past it is not.
  • No ball struck — the cue ball reached nothing at all.
  • Wrong ball first — the first contact was not from your group, or was not the 8 once your group was already clear.
  • No rail after contact — you hit the right ball, then nothing dropped and nothing reached a cushion.
  • Struck the 8 on an open table — the 8 is nobody's ball until a group has been cleared.

A foul never assigns a group, and potting your own ball on a foul does not rescue the shot — the turn passes anyway. Nothing that goes down is ever returned to the table.

Ending the rack — the 8

  • You may shoot at the 8 only once every ball of your group was already down when you struck. Clearing your last ball and the 8 on the same shot is a loss.
  • A winning 8 is a clean one: the 8 struck first, potted, no foul.
  • Pot the 8 early, foul while potting it, or scratch on it, and the opponent wins immediately.
  • Potting your last group ball is an ordinary legal pot. You stay at the table, now shooting the 8.

House calls

Where this table is deliberately friendlier than the rule book:

  • Nothing is called. No naming the ball, no naming the pocket. A ball that drops, counts — slop included, and so are combinations, caroms and kicks, so long as you hit your own first.
  • The 8 on the break is a rerack, where most rule sets make it a win or a loss.
  • The break has no rail requirement. Any break is a legal break.
  • No three-foul rule, and no push-out after the break.
  • Ball in hand is anywhere on every foul but the scratch.
